Who this fits

It works best for an owner-run heating and air shop, from a handful of people up to about twenty, where the office still runs on paper, spreadsheets and text messages, and most of it runs through you.

You keep the accounting and payroll software you already use. This handles what happens before the numbers get there: the quote, the job, the hours, and what the job really cost.

It isn't an app you download and figure out alone. I set it up with you, around the way your shop already quotes, runs jobs and tracks hours, and you deal with me directly.

Plainly: it doesn't take card payments or send invoices, and it doesn't connect to your accounting or payroll software. If you need those built in today, this isn't the right fit, and I'll tell you so on the call.

Three problems, and what fixing them looks like

Problem 1

Quotes that go quiet

You drive out, write it up, and nobody follows up.

Every open quote sits in one list, and the office gets told which ones are about to expire. The customer can pick an option and accept online.

Problem 2

Friday time cards

Chasing a stack of paper and texts every week.

Each tech fills in the week, a supervisor approves it, and it prints ready for payroll.

Problem 3

Did the job make money?

Usually you find out when the accountant tells you.

On a project, you see the budget, the parts and the hours side by side.
